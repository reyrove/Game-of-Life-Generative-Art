00 / Statement Artist Statement

Four rules. No designer. Yet the grid keeps producing form.

In 1970, John Horton Conway defined a zero-player game: each cell on a grid lives, dies or is born according to its eight neighbours. Nothing about the rule-set is visual — and yet the resulting patterns are among the most recognisable textures in computational art.

Game of Life is a companion volume to Arachne — the same interest in rule-based surface, approached through a different engine. Where Arachne layers rotating rings, this works cell by cell, generation by generation, until a stable, oscillating or travelling pattern emerges.

Each composition begins from a random seed, a random grid, and a randomly paired palette. The structure is entirely emergent — never drawn, always produced.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimisation. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Seed

Variation

A random seed initialises the grid size, the palette pairing, and the initial cell density. Each composition is regenerated and documented as a distinct output.

02 / Evolution

Geometry

Four rules applied to every cell — birth, survival, death. The grid evolves through generations until a stable, oscillating or travelling pattern emerges.
